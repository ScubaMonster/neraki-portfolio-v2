01_THE_CONTEXT

The trip is exciting. Preparing for it often is not.

Packing looks like a simple checklist task until destination, duration, weather, activities, luggage limits and personal needs begin competing for attention.

Generic lists can offer a starting point, but they still leave travellers to decide what applies, what is missing and how much of everything they actually need.

02_THE_GOAL

Turn travel details into a plan that feels personal.

Packing Assistant aims to ask only the questions that matter, generate a useful first draft and keep every recommendation transparent and editable.

The assistant should reduce remembering—not remove the traveller’s control.

03_THE_CHALLENGE

One suitcase can contain dozens of small decisions.

The concept focuses on the moments where preparation becomes repetitive, uncertain or easy to forget.

01

Mental overload

Travellers have to remember essentials while also planning transport, accommodation, activities and timing.

02

Generic advice

Standard packing lists rarely account for the specific trip, traveller, routine or luggage restrictions.

03

Changing conditions

Weather, plans and trip details can change after the first list is made, so the plan needs to adapt without starting again.

04_EARLY_DEFINITION

Turning a familiar routine into a decision system.

I began by unpacking how I already make packing decisions: identify what every trip needs, then add or adjust items only when the trip context makes them relevant.

05_RESEARCH_NOTES

The first artefact was the logic—not the interface.

These handwritten notes capture the project selection, initial categories and the first version of the recommendation rules.

KEY_LOGIC_CORRECTION

Destination and season cannot be treated as shortcuts for one another.

A beach trip can happen in winter, and a mountain trip can happen in summer. Destination determines activity-specific items; season determines weather weight and layers. Keeping those rules separate prevents confident but irrelevant advice.

06_THE_DECISION_MODEL

Build from a stable base, then add only what applies.

The concept does not begin with one enormous master list. It combines four understandable rule layers, then gives the traveller the final say over the generated checklist.

  • Begin with documents, chargers, toiletries and medication
  • Add trip-type items for business or leisure
  • Add season and activity-specific destination items
  • Adjust clothing quantities from the trip duration

07_NEXT_VALIDATION_STEPS

What still needs to be tested—not disguised as finished.

The rule system and product direction are defined. The next phase is to find out whether the questions, explanations and controls work for people beyond my own packing routine.

01

Compare routines

Interview travellers with different trip types, needs and packing habits.

02

Prototype the questions

Test progressive setup, recommendation reasons and editable list controls.

03

Test edge cases

Challenge combinations such as winter beaches, summer mountains and mixed-purpose trips.

08_DESIGN_PRINCIPLES

Three rules for a genuinely helpful assistant.

01

Ask with purpose.

Every question should visibly improve the list. If it does not, the traveller should not have to answer it.

02

Explain suggestions.

Recommendations should connect to the trip details that produced them, especially weather and planned activities.

03

Keep people in control.

Generated does not mean fixed. Every category and item should remain easy to edit, remove or personalise.

MISSION_STATUS // IN_PROGRESS

The foundation is packed. The product is not finished.

The problem, core rules and initial product direction are now documented. Broader user research, interface prototyping and usability testing remain the next stages of the project.
