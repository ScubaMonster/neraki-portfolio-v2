01_THE_STORY

A small mouse with a New Orleans-sized dream.

Blue Notes follows a small orphaned mouse who has always dreamed of playing the saxophone in New Orleans. Standing on the Mississippi riverbank, he can finally see the city's lights—but the wide river remains one last, enormous obstacle.

Nearby, a turtle is enjoying a green cocktail when she hears him play the blues. After a startled introduction, she offers to carry him across herself: although she is a land animal, she is a swimmer on her father's side. She asks for no reward—only that he never stops playing the blues.

02_THE_WORLD

A tiny city built to hold a very human feeling.

Light transformed handmade materials into the distant promise of New Orleans—and made the Mississippi feel even wider.

03_VISUAL_LANGUAGE

Texture, light and colour carry the emotion.

Every visual choice supports the scale of the mouse's dream, the river before him and the warmth of unexpected kindness.

01

Visible texture

Crochet, fabric and paper remain visibly handmade, giving the characters warmth and vulnerability.

02

Midnight atmosphere

Deep blue space and isolated pools of light turn the set into an intimate stage.

03

Rhythm through motion

Small poses and frame-by-frame changes allow personality to emerge without dialogue.

04_HANDMADE_PROCESS

Built by hand. Moved frame by frame.

Stop motion turns physical craft into performance. Characters, props, lighting and camera placement had to work together before each movement could be captured and shaped in the edit.

  1. 01

    Shape the story into a clear visual sequence.

  2. 02

    Build the characters with expressive silhouettes and movable forms.

  3. 03

    Construct the world from small-scale sets, props and practical light.

  4. 04

    Animate each action through patient, incremental changes.

  5. 05

    Edit the frames into rhythm, atmosphere and emotional continuity.

05_FRAMES_FROM_THE_FILM

“Never stop playing the blues.”

06_REFLECTION

Patience became part of the storytelling.

Blue Notes taught me that emotional clarity can grow from the smallest decisions: the angle of a head, the pause between two movements, the colour of a light or the distance between a dreamer and the city he hopes to reach.

The process strengthened my patience, planning and ability to think across disciplines—from character and set design to animation, photography and editing—while keeping one central feeling intact.
