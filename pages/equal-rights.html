MISSION_04 // VISUAL_COMMUNICATION

Equal Rights Campaign

Helping a movement become immediately recognisable.

A visual identity created for a marriage-equality campaign competition organised by Antivirus Magazine. The concept placed second and was designed to communicate recognition, unity and equal belonging with clarity and optimism.

01_THE_MISSION

Design a symbol people could recognise, remember and stand behind.

The project was not simply about producing an attractive logo. It needed to support a public message: equal relationships deserve equal recognition.

The identity therefore had to feel direct enough for campaign use, warm enough to invite connection and flexible enough to remain legible across different formats.

CAMPAIGN DOSSIER
Objective
Create a memorable identity supporting marriage equality.
Audience
A broad public audience across campaign touchpoints.
Message
Equality, recognition, unity and shared belonging.
Constraint
Communicate quickly without relying on a long explanation.

02_CONCEPT_EXPLORATION

Searching for the simplest expression of a complex idea.

This section is built for the real sketches, alternatives and rejected directions.

03_DESIGN_DECISIONS

Every element had to support the same message.

Shape

The symbol needed to read quickly, remain balanced and suggest connection rather than separation.

Typography

The lettering needed enough presence for campaign use while remaining clear and accessible.

Colour

Colour supported optimism and visibility without overwhelming the core identity.

Symbolism

The concept focused on shared dignity and recognition rather than decoration alone.

04_FINAL_IDENTITY

Give the final mark space to speak.

The visual centre of this page belongs to the real identity. Once the artwork is added, this section should remain intentionally calm.

05_VISUAL_SYSTEM

A small identity system built for recognition.

The logo is the centre, but a campaign identity also depends on consistent spacing, colour, typography and repeatable use.

  • Primary identity
  • Compact or alternate treatment
  • Colour and monochrome versions
  • Clear-space and minimum-size guidance

06_IN_CONTEXT

A campaign identity has to live beyond the artboard.

Use real outputs where available. Mockups should only demonstrate plausible applications of the genuine identity.

07_RECOGNITION

Second Place

Marriage Equality Campaign Competition

Organised by Antivirus Magazine. The recognition validated the concept's ability to communicate the campaign message clearly and memorably.

08_REFLECTION

Visual identity is not decoration. It becomes a shared signal.

Designing for a cause changed the weight of every decision. A symbol can help people recognise an idea, but it must also treat that idea with care.

This project strengthened my understanding of reduction: removing anything that did not support the message until the remaining elements could communicate with confidence.
